Bathroom humidity · status light

Answers one question: did someone shower and leave the window shut? The trick is that a normal shower and a stuck one look identical at their peak — what separates them is whether the humidity comes back down. So the light reads the shape of the curve over time, not how high it got.

Everything is measured against a rolling baseline — the 25th percentile of the last 24 hours — so "elevated" means elevated for this week. A humid Tel Aviv summer and a dry winter both work with no retuning.

The decision

Latest reading + 24 h baseline
one sample every ~30 s · 6 h of history in hand
↓
Is it above baseline + 5 pts?
is the room damp at all right now?
──▸NO GREEN · NORMAL
↓YES
Did it get there via a sharp rise?
≥ 6 pts within 15 min — a shower, not a slow drift
──▸NO GREEN · SLOW DRIFT
↓YES
Elevated for less than 30 min?
grace period — a vented room clears in 10–20 min
──▸YES AMBER · DRYING
↓NO
Is it still coming down?
recovered ≥ 50 % from the peak, or falling ≥ 1.5 pts in 10 min
──▸YES AMBER · SLOW BUT CLEARING
↓NO — IT HAS STALLED
RED · STUCK — “OPEN THE WINDOW”

The knobs

ConstantValueWhat it decides
HUM_ELEVATED_MARGIN5 pts How far above baseline counts as damp. Too low and normal fluctuation trips it.
HUM_RISE_DELTA6 pts Size of jump that marks a shower — this is what keeps slow damp out of it.
HUM_RISE_WINDOW_MIN15 min How fast that jump has to happen.
HUM_STUCK_MIN30 min Grace period before a shower is even considered a problem.
HUM_CLEARING_FRAC0.5 How much of the peak must be recovered to still count as clearing. The main amber↔red dial.
HUM_CLEARING_DROP1.5 pts / 10 min Fallback: falling this fast counts as clearing even if not much recovered yet.

Worked example — a real reading

04 Sep, 13:38 · amber
Baseline 60.4 % → elevated starts at 65.4 %. Current 67.4 %, so it is elevated.
It arrived via a spike to 74.7 %, so the light is armed.
Elevated for 43 min — past the 30 min grace, so now the trend decides.
Recovered (74.7 − 67.4) ÷ (74.7 − 60.4) = 51 % — just over the 50 % line, so it reads as still clearing → amber.
One point the other way and this would have been red. That borderline is exactly the case to watch as more showers accumulate.
Implemented as humidity_status() in server.py — a pure function, so HumidityStatusTest can exercise it against synthetic traces (quiet · clearing · stuck · still-falling · no-spike drift · winter baseline). Decided server-side because the rolling baseline needs 24 h of history, far more than the board is sent.
Thresholds were derived from the sensor's own data and are provisional — worth revisiting once a few weeks of real showers exist to check them against.
